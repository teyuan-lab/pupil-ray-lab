READ THE RAYS
Model, pupil calculation & sources

Sequential intersections with spherical surfaces; vector Snell refraction in air and homogeneous glass. A numerical shooting solve aims each field ray at a chosen height in the active aperture stop. The chief ray targets its center. Solid segments are actual rays; dashed segments extend rays to a pupil image.

Pupils are Gaussian images of the active on-axis aperture stop, calculated with infinitesimal rays traced through the same surfaces in each direction. Finite rays exhibit pupil aberration, so they need not cross exactly at the paraxial pupil. The active stop is identified automatically from paraxial on-axis collimated-ray acceptance at all clear apertures. The adjustable diaphragm may cease to be the stop. Other apertures can still vignette off-axis or finite-aperture beams; this is reported. Eye relief is the signed distance from the last vertex to the paraxial exit pupil; only a real external pupil is accessible.

Illustrative spherical singlets, monochromatic n, meridional rays. No diffraction, coating losses or dispersion. The reduced eye uses one exact spherical refracting surface for combined cornea/lens power: n = 4/3, fixed 57 D, retina 22.22 mm behind the vertex. Eye power is fixed; retina position stays fixed. The physical iris/pupil is 3 mm behind the equivalent front surface. Rays refract first and are then clipped at that internal pupil. Eye position is referenced to its paraxial entrance pupil (the apparent image of the iris through the front surface), which aligns with the instrument exit pupil at zero offset. The close-up camera pans with the eye/exit-pupil region using equal, fixed horizontal/vertical scales; the eyeball never stretches. A spherical retina of radius 12 mm receives three-dimensional rays from the asymmetric F target; image coordinates are its transverse projection. This is an illustrative reduced eye, not an anatomical prescription. Reduced-eye model reference (NUS). Changing index re-spaces lenses for paraxial focus. Defocus changes only the final ocular position. The eyepiece preset includes an upstream objective to define its input pupil.
